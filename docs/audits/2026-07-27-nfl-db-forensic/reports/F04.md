**Volume policy, stated so it can be checked.** The contract's volume rule is applied as follows:
`MATCH` collapses into the row line via `fields_compared`/`fields_matched`; *structural*
`NOT_COMPARABLE` (same reason for every row of the table — EPA is a model output, ESPN has no
position column, …) collapses into `fields_not_comparable` and is enumerated once per table in
`cache/f04/internal/not_comparable_manifest.json`; **every** `MISMATCH`, `DB_ONLY`, `REF_ONLY`,
`UNRESOLVED`, and every *row-specific* `NOT_COMPARABLE` gets its own line. A row-level line is
emitted for **every** row, not only clean ones, and its verdict is `NOT_COMPARABLE` iff the
authority could rule on nothing (`fields_compared == 0`), otherwise the worst problem verdict.

## 2. The `LA` ambiguity — my independent verdict

**`LA` is unambiguously the Rams (franchise 14) in this database, in both seasons, in every table.
I tested it four ways that do not depend on `team_alias`, and no test found a single row where `LA`
resolves to the Chargers.** The prior agent's conclusion holds, but its stated basis
(game-anchored rows) is narrower than the evidence now available.

**Test 1 — ESPN's era-correct franchise identity** (`seasons/{y}/teams/{id}`, 64 payloads under
`cache/f04/teammeta/`). ESPN's seasonal team endpoint *is* era-correct, which makes it a real
authority across the two relocations:

| | franchise 14 | franchise 24 |
|---|---|---|
| ESPN 2016 | `LAR` — Los Angeles Rams | `SD` — **San Diego** Chargers |
| ESPN 2017 | `LAR` — Los Angeles Rams | `LAC` — **Los Angeles** Chargers |
| DB `team` | `LAR` — Los Angeles Rams | `LAC` — Los Angeles Chargers |

Both moves are represented correctly and both franchise ids are stable across them.

**Test 2 — every `LA`/`LAC`/`SD` label in `game`, against the ESPN competitor id on the same row.**
`franchise_id` shares ESPN's id space, so this is a direct test, not an alias lookup:

```
2016   LA -> {14: 16}      SD  -> {24: 16}
2017   LA -> {14: 17}      LAC -> {24: 16}
```

`LA` maps to franchise 14 on 33 of 33 occurrences and to nothing else. `SD` (2016) and `LAC`
(2017) map to franchise 24 on 32 of 32. **No bare `LA` is ever used for the Chargers**, including
in 2017 when both clubs played in Los Angeles. Every one of the 534 games' `away_franchise_id` and
`home_franchise_id` was compared to the ESPN competitor team id: 1,068 of 1,068 `MATCH`.

**Test 3 — 1,978 per-athlete club attributions in `player_game_stats`.** This is a finer test than
the game-level one: each row carries its own `franchise_id`, and ESPN's box score independently
assigns each individual athlete to a team id, so a club mix-up would show up player by player.
Agreement over the two Los Angeles franchises:

```
db 14 -> espn 14 : 989 rows      db 24 -> espn 24 : 989 rows
db 14 -> espn 24 :   0 rows      db 24 -> espn 14 :   0 rows
```

Zero cross-assignments in 1,978 opportunities, spanning 2017 when both clubs were in Los Angeles
simultaneously.

**Test 4 — Pro-Football-Reference's independent club codes, embedded in `pfr_game_id`.** PFR uses
`ram` for the Rams and keeps `sdg` for the Chargers *after* the move (e.g. `201709170sdg` is a
2017 Chargers home game at StubHub Center). Parsing all 534 `pfr_game_id` values as
`YYYYMMDD + home club code` and checking each against the DB's `home_franchise_id` and `gameday`:
**534 of 534 MATCH.** A confusion of the two Los Angeles clubs would be visible here as `ram`/`sdg`
swaps; there are none.

**Corroborating observation on the ingest sources.** The raw nflverse extracts do *not* agree with
each other on the 2016 Chargers label — `player_stats.csv` retro-normalises them to `LAC` (513
rows) while `snap_counts.csv`, `rosters.csv` and `depth_charts.csv` use the era-correct `SD`. Both
labels resolve to franchise 24 through `team_alias`, so the divergence is absorbed correctly, but
it is worth knowing that a naive string join on the raw feeds would split the 2016 Chargers in two.

**One convention divergence, not an identity error** (see §3.2): the DB stores nflverse's `LA` for
the Rams where ESPN publishes `LAR`. 33 rows. The franchise id on those same rows is verified
correct.

## 5. `game_line` — 534 rows

Authority per contract: **covers.com / SportsOddsHistory**, which publishes the favourite, the
spread magnitude, the score and the total **and nothing else** — no moneyline, no juice. Those six
price columns are therefore structurally `NOT_COMPARABLE` against the named authority. ESPN's
multi-book odds feed (534 payloads, 10-13 books per event) is used as a corroborating second
source, compared in **implied-probability space** (American odds are discontinuous across ±100, so
raw arithmetic on them is meaningless).

### 5.1 `spread_line` / `total_line` vs SportsOddsHistory

**All 534 games matched to a SportsOddsHistory row** (verified: 534 matched, 0 unmatched — the
neutral-site date-drift fallback never fired). `spread_line` agrees on 482, `total_line` on 491.

| delta (db − SOH) | spread_line | total_line |
|---|---:|---:|
| −2.0 | 2 | — |
| −1.0 | 4 | 2 |
| −0.5 | 15 | 11 |
| 0 | **482** | **491** |
| +0.5 | 25 | 28 |
| +1.0 | 2 | 2 |
| +1.5 | 1 | — |
| +2.0 | 3 | — |

Half-point drift between two odds archives is ordinary. What matters is the **favourite side**.

### 5.2 Five favourite-side disagreements — the material finding of this partition

Five rows where the DB and SOH disagree about *which club is favoured* (all near pick'em, |line| 1).
For each I recorded ESPN's books as a third source and did **not** pick a winner:

| Game | DB | SOH | ESPN books | DB's own moneyline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `2016_04_IND_JAX` | JAX −1 | IND −1 | 8 say IND, 3 say JAX | −104/−106 — **byte-identical to ESPN `teamrankings`, which also says JAX** |
| `2017_03_CLE_IND` | IND −1 | CLE −1 | 8 say CLE, 2 say IND | +105/−116 — **byte-identical to `teamrankings`, which also says IND** |
| `2017_05_SF_IND` | SF −1 | IND −1 | 9 say IND, 1 says SF | −104/−106 — **byte-identical to `teamrankings`, which also says SF** |
| `2017_11_ATL_SEA` | SEA −1 | ATL −1 | 9 say ATL, 1 says SEA | +103/−114 — no book twin; closest is `accuscore` (SEA −1, −105/−116) |
| `2017_14_PHI_LA` | PHI −1 | LAR −1 | 7 say PHI, 3 say LAR | −115/+104 — **majority of books side with the DB; SOH is the outlier here** |

The pattern is precise and reproducible: on three of the five, nflverse's stored price pair is
byte-identical to ESPN's `teamrankings` feed, and `teamrankings` assigns the favourite the same way
the DB does while the rest of the market assigns it the other way. So this is not random noise —
it is one upstream feed's favourite-assignment convention on ~1-point games, inherited whole. On
the fifth (`2017_14_PHI_LA`) the DB agrees with the market and SOH is wrong. **Impact:** on these
five games `team_game.ats_result` and `covered` are computed from a spread whose sign the wider
market disputes; a backtest will score them opposite to how most books settled them.
`2017_05_SF_IND` was already flagged by A4 EX-08; the other four are new.

### 5.4 `2017_04_CHI_GB` — the only manually supplied odds row

Verified in full, independently.

**The flag is correct and correctly isolated.** `SELECT game_id, odds_source FROM game_line
JOIN game USING(game_id) WHERE season IN (2016,2017) AND odds_source <> 'nflverse'` returns
**exactly one row**: `2017_04_CHI_GB`, `odds_source = 'manual-2026-07-27'`. All 533 other rows are
`nflverse`. **No other row in my two seasons was silently patched.**

**Nothing was overwritten.** `nflverse/nfldata/data/games.csv` for `2017_04_CHI_GB` publishes
`spread_line=7.5`, `total_line=44`, and **empty strings** for all six price columns. The manual row
filled six blanks; it did not replace six values.

**`spread_line = 7.5` and `total_line = 44` are byte-identical to nflverse** and both are quoted by
ESPN books for the event: `GB -7.5` by Unibet, Westgate, Wynn and teamrankings; `44.0` by
CG Technology, Wynn, numberfire and teamrankings. (The ESPN `consensus` and the other five books
say `GB -7` / `44.5`, so 7.5/44 is a real quoted line, not the modal one.)

**ESPN independently corroborates +311 / −357 — confirmed here from my own cached payload**
(`cache/f04/odds/odds_400951678.json.gz`):

```
teamrankings : GB -7.5 | O/U 44.0 | away +311 | home -357   <- exactly the two stored values
Unibet       : GB -7.5 | O/U 44.5 | away +285 | home -357   <- home side independently -357
consensus    : GB -7   | O/U 44.5 | away +313 | home -389
```

`teamrankings` reproduces **both** moneylines exactly and Unibet reproduces the home side. The two
moneylines are sourced.

**The four −110 juice values remain assumed, and I can now bound the assumption.** ESPN publishes
over/under odds of exactly −110/−110 at five of the nine real books for this event (CG Technology,
Caesar's, Caesars Sportsbook, Westgate, Wynn), with Unibet at −104/−114, accuscore at −105/−116,
consensus at −106/−112 and teamrankings at −108/−102. So −110/−110 for over/under is the modal
market price and that half of the assumption is well supported. **Spread juice is a different story:** no ESPN book
quotes −110/−110 for this event's spread. The quoted pairs are −105/−115, +100/−120 (×2),
−127/+108, −120/+100, −125/+105, −105/−116, −124/+112. The stored `away_spread_odds = −110` and
`home_spread_odds = −110` are **not** quoted by any book here and sit inside a range whose books
consistently price the two sides asymmetrically on a 7.5-point line. The four values are correctly
described as assumed standard juice and must not be treated as observed prices; the two spread-side
ones are the weaker pair. Filter `odds_source = 'nflverse'` for any juice-sensitive work — and note
A4's warning still applies: 108 plain `nflverse` rows carry the same all-−110 pattern, so the value
is not a marker; `odds_source` is the only discriminator.
